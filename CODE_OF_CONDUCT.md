# Code of conduct

## The point of this

Posnic is used by shopkeepers, and built largely by people who are not
professional programmers writing in their first language. A project that is
unpleasant to participate in loses exactly the contributors it most needs: the
person who found a real bug but is not certain how to describe it.

So the standard is simple. **Be someone people are willing to bring a problem
to.**

## What that looks like

- **Assume the other person is trying.** A vague bug report is usually someone
  doing their best with a shop to run, not someone wasting your time.
- **Criticise the code, not the person.** "This throws when the branch is
  missing" is useful. "Did you even test this?" is not.
- **English is not everyone's first language.** Read for meaning. Do not correct
  someone's grammar unless it is in the product.
- **Say why.** "We will not do that" without a reason is a poor answer even when
  it is the correct one.
- **Accept that people go quiet.** Nearly everyone here is doing this alongside a
  job. Slow is not rude.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks
- Discrimination on any grounds — including caste, religion, region and
  language, which matter where much of this community is
- Sexual attention or imagery, anywhere in project spaces
- Publishing anyone's private information without permission
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, reopening settled decisions to
  relitigate them, demanding unpaid work
- Deliberately submitting malicious code, or anything that puts a real shop's
  data at risk

## Where this applies

Every project space: issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages and
code review, and anywhere someone is representing the project. It also applies
to behaviour elsewhere when it directly affects someone's safety or their
ability to take part here.

## Reporting

Email **info@posnic.com**. If your report concerns the person who reads that
address, say so and it will be handled by someone else.

Include what happened, where, when, and links if you have them. You do not need
to prove anything or write it formally.

**What we do:**

- Acknowledge within **3 working days**
- Keep your identity confidential from the person reported, unless you agree
  otherwise
- Hear the other side before acting
- Tell you the outcome

**What we will not do** is quietly take a side, or ask you to settle it
privately with someone who made you uncomfortable.

Reporting in good faith never counts against you, even if we conclude nothing
happened. Retaliating against a reporter is itself a serious breach.

## What happens

Roughly in order, starting at the lowest step that fits:

1. **A private word.** Most things end here — usually a misunderstanding or a
   bad day.
2. **A public correction.** A comment naming the behaviour and what is expected.
3. **A temporary ban** from project spaces, with a stated length and the
   conditions for returning.
4. **A permanent ban.** For sustained behaviour after warnings, or a single
   severe act — harassment, threats, or deliberately putting shops' data at risk.

Decisions are made by the maintainers, currently as described in
[GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md#where-the-project-is-today). If you believe one
was wrong, say so at the same address; where a second maintainer exists, they
review it.

## A note on tone

Technical disagreement is welcome and expected. "This is the wrong approach, and
here is why" is not a conduct matter — it is the job.

The line is between the work and the person. Cross it and someone will say so,
which is the mechanism working rather than failing.
